## 2. Epoxides
- In bromination, the bromide is a sufficiently good nucleophile to attack the bromonium ion.
	- This is not always the case.
	- With poor nucleophiles, molecules like epoxides form.
- To yield an epoxide we react a peracid with an alkene.
	- Peracids have the general structure R-CO<sub>3</sub>H.
		- It's like a carboxylic acid, but instead of the C-OH bond, it has a C-O-OH bond.
			- In this the hydrogen has a partial positive charge, so it acts as the electrophile.
		- A commonly used one for epoxide formation is m-CPBA, meta-chloroperoxybenzoic acid.
- A general reaction involves:
	1. Nucleophile attacks the OH on peracid.
	2. It gains the hydroxy group, while forming the anion.
### 2.1. Mechanism
- The mechanism is **concerted**, meaning that all bond breaking and making occurs in **one** step.
	-  The π-electrons in the alkene C=C bond attack the peracid.
	- The negative charge on the oxygen is moved to the carbonyl oxygen
	- This charged oxygen attacks the proton on the -OH transferred, forming an O-H bond.
	- As a result, the negatively charged O will attack the other side of the C-C bond, forming the epoxide ring.
#### 2.1.1. Stereochemistry
- The stereochemistry is again conserved.
	- Conformer orientation same as geometric isomers
- However, with trans-alkenes, the result is a racemic mix of two stereoisomers, because the attack can occur from any side.
### 2.2 Epoxides' ring-opening
#### 2.2.1. In acid with water
- We can convert epoxides to diols using an aqueous solution of acid.
	1. Like most reactions we have seen, the reaction begins with protonation of epoxide oxygen.
		- Notice from here that the reaction is similar to bromination.
	2. The water then attacks a carbon.
	3. The water is deprotonated.
#### 2.2.2. In base with methanol
- We can also use a sodium methoxide, NaOMe solution in methanol.
	- This attacks at the **less substituted carbon**.
		- This is due to steric hindrance.
- This yields a hydroxyether **after acid work-up**.
#### 2.2.3. In acid with methanol
- We can do this in acid with methanol.
	- The methanol, not methoxide attacks in this case.
- The methanol is deprotonated to give a hydroxyether again.
## 3. Diols
- We now know how to synthesise a trans-diol from alkene
	- Epoxide formation with peracid
	- Ring-opening with acid.
- We make cis-diols using osmium tetroxide, OsO<sub>4</sub>.
	- The molecule is essentially four Os=O bonds.
	1. The π-electrons attack the O, pushing the negative charge onto the Os.
	2. This pushes a neighbouring Os=O to attack the other end of the C-C bond.
		- This form an osmate ester.
	3. The compound is then hydrolysed with acid to give cis diols.
### 3.1. Acid-catalysed hydrolysis of osmate ester
1. Again and again, we protonate an oxygen from one of the two C-O-Os bonds.
2. The Os-O bond breaks, giving the Os a positive charge.
3. The water acts as the nucleophile, so a proton is regenerated.
4. The process repeats with the other C-O-Os bond.
- The products are:
	- **cis**-diol
	- Os(VI)
> **NOTE** Osmium tetroxide is expensive, so a co-oxidiser is often added to re-oxidise back to VIII.
