## 2. Aromaticity
- We can determine is a molecule is aromatic using Huckel's rule (See CH164).
- We can also have nitrogen-containing aromatic compounds.
	- But their behaviour is different.
- In pyridine:
	- The nitrogen's lone pair is in sp<sup>2</sup> orbital, so it is not delocalised around the ring.
- In pyrolle:
	- The nitrogen lone pair is in unhybridised p-orbital, so it is delocalised around the ring.
- They do this to minimise the energy of the molecule.

## 3. Electrophilic aromatic substitution
- We cannot directly electrophilically add to benzene.
	- The aromatic stabilisation is very strong.
- So we use compounds such as FeBr<sub>3</sub> together with bromine to make the electrophile more electrophilic.
	- This acts as as a better electrophile after formation of a Br-Br<sup>+</sup>-Fe<sup>-</sup>Br<sub>3</sub>.
	- This is the **activated electrophile**.
### 3.1. General mechanism
- Not all details are required, but:
	1. A π-electron from the benzene attacks the electrophile.
	2. The electrophile is added to one side while a carbocation is formed on the other side.
	3. The C-H bond breaks, restoring aromaticity.
	- Step 1 is the RDS
- For bromine we use FeBr<sub>3</sub> in bromine, and for chlorine we use AlCl<sub>3</sub> in chlorine.
#### 3.1.1. Nitration
- We can add a nitro group (R-NO<sub>2</sub>) to the benzene too.
	- It involves HNO<sub>3</sub> in H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub>.
	1. The nitric acid's hydroxyl group is protonated by sulphuric acid
	2. The hydroxyl group leaves as water, negatively-charged oxygen forms C=O bond.
	3. So this forms a positively charged O=N<sup>+</sup>=O nitronium ion.
#### 3.1.2. Friedel-Crafts alkylation
- Essentially the same mechanism, but we use a chloroalkane with AlCl<sub>3</sub>.
	1. The Cl on chloroalkane attacks the AlCl<sub>3</sub>.
	2. The C-Cl bond is then broken, because the chlorine now has a positive charge.
	3. This form a carbocation and [AlCl<sub>4</sub>]<sup>-</sup>.
#### 3.1.3. Friedel-Crafts acylation
- Same as alkylation, but a acid chloride and AlCl<sub>3</sub> is used.
	- This yields an acyl carbocation  electrophile, which has a C-(triple bond)-O resonance form.
