## 2. Electrophilic substitution of substituted aryls
### 2.1. Reactivity
- The substituents can affect the reactivity.
- Consider these aromatic compounds:

| Compound       | Relative Rate     |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| Benzene        | 1                 |
| Methoxybenzene | 1x10<sup>9</sup>  |
| Toluene        | 4x10<sup>3</sup>  |
| Nitrobenzene   | 1x10<sup>-7</sup> |
- The difference arises from EDG and EWGs.
	- Methoxy and methyl groups are EDGs, so they donate into the benzene ring to make it more electrophilic.
		- They are **activating**.
	- Nitro group is EWG. It withdraws electron density from the benzene ring to make it less electronegative.
		- They are **deactivating**.
### 2.2. Location of substitution
- Relative to the first electrophile substitutes, we can add to the ortho (adjacent), meta (one apart), and para (opposite) positions.
- As a general rule:
	- EWG, deactivating groups direct to the **meta** position.
	- EDG, activating groups direct to the ortho and para position.
#### 2.2.1. Inductive and mesomeric effects
- Groups like CF<sub>3</sub> are inductively withdrawing.
- Groups like Me are inductively donating.
- Groups like NO<sub>2</sub> are resonance withdrawing. 
- Groups like MeO are resonance donating.
### 2.3. Substitution of phenol and aniline
- OH is resonance donating, so it is activating and the resulting phenol can react directly with bromine.
	- This is a very fast reaction to give 2,4,6-tribromophenol
- The preference comes from the resonance donation of the OH leading to negative charges being localised on the ortho and para positions.
- The order of substitution is para -> ortho -> ortho.
### 2.4. Substitution of alkyl benzenes
- We just saw that resonance donating groups react very quickly and undergo substitution thrice.
- For inductively donating groups this is not the case.
- They only undergo one further substitution.
- For alkyl benzenes, the rough distributions are:

| O   | M   | P   |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 60  | 5   | 35  |
- So major product is ortho, followed by para.
	- They are major products due to the positive charge being present on the carbon which is bonded to the methyl group.
	- This provides stabilisation via hyperconjugation to the carbocation, stabilising them more than para, which only forms carbocations on the adjacent carbons.
- The reason that ortho is more major than para, is because there are two orthos.
	- There may be some steric hindrance from the alkyl;
	- But this is not as large of an effect as statistical likelihood.
- The reaction for O and P is faster too as a result.
### 2.5. EWGs
- EWGs reduce the reactivity of the benzene group, so different reagents are required.
	- To nitrate, fuming HNO<sub>3</sub> and concentrated H<sub>2</sub>SO<sub>4</sub> will be required.
	- The reaction mixture also needs to be heated to 100 degrees.
- They are meta-directing.
	- This is due to the ortho and para positions generating a positive charge adjacent to the EWG. (See above)
	- So the carbocations for o and p are destabilised compared to m.
