## 2. Electrophilic substitution of substituted aryls (cont.)
- Electrophilic substitution occurs for halobenzenes too.
- The results are:

| O   | M   | P   |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 43% | 1%  | 56% |
- This is because while halogens can donate electron density from their LPs, their high electronegativity leads them to hold on to the electrons.
	- So we classify them as EWG.
- Nonetheless, with ortho and para, the carbocation can still be stabilised by the electrons.
	- Here the distribution is different to alkyl benzene because halogens are quite large, and they will have more steric hindrance than alkyl groups.
- The reagents are the same, FeBr<sub>3</sub> in bromine.
## 3. Chemistry of the nitro group
- Nitro groups are versatile. They are:
	- Easily introduced with nitration (EWG)
	- Reduced to amine (EDG)
- Consider a benzene molecule.
	- If we nitrated then brominated:
		- The m-directing properties of nitro group will result in m-bromoaniline.
	- If we brominated then nitrated:
		- The p-directing properties of bromine will result in p-bromoaniline.
- So the order of reaction is important too.
