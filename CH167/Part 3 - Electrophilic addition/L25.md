## 2. Hydrobromination
- We can add HBr to alkene.
	1. The π-electrons attack the hydrogen, forming a carbocation.
	2. The bromide adds to the carbocation.
- The general rules stated before still holds:
	- The more substituted sp<sup>2</sub> carbon is more nucleophilic.
		- The carbocation formed is more stable too.
## 3. Markovnikov's Rule
> **When HX is added to an alkene, H adds to the carbon with least alkyl groups and X adds to the carbon with most alkyl groups**.

> **NOTE** This does not apply for symmetrical alkenes like but-2-ene or ethene, as the results are identical regardless of the side Br is added.
- The real cause of Markovnikov's is due to carbocation stability.
	- When H is added a carbocation intermdiate forms.
	- On a more substituted alkene the carbocation is more stable.
> **NOTE** The same applies for conjugated carbocations. Conjugation stabilisation is also a factor to consider.
> You can remember this rule as 'The rich gets richer'.
## 4. Hydration
- Markovnikov's rule applies here too.
- This occurs in an aqueous solution of acids.
	- The elimination of water is favoured at high temperatures.
- The reverse, acid hydrolysis, is E1.
	- This is favoured at higher temperatures.
### 3.1. Anti-Markovnikov addition of water
- Water can be added opposite to Markovnikov.
	- The alkene is reacted with borane, BH<sub>3</sub>.
	- This gives a trialkyl borane.
	- It is then oxidised with hydrogen peroxide and sodium hydroxide
	- This gives an **anti-Markovnikov** product.
- This is the **Hydroboration-oxidation sequence**.
> **NOTE** This is a concerted mechanism (see L24)
#### 3.1.1. Formation of trialkyl borane
- Consider a B-H bond.
	- H is slightly more electronegative, so it will add to the more substituted sp<sup>2</sup>.
	- B will add to the less substituted.
	- So an alkane with one side H and the other BH<sub>2</sub> forms.
- This process repeats to give the trialkyl borane, which will need to oxidised.
> **NOTE** Because of the B-H bond, there is only one cis-diastereomer. It is not anti.
#### 3.1.2. Oxidation of trialkyl borane
- This step is done with NaOH in hydrogen peroxide.
	1. The hydroperoxide (HO<sub>2</sub><sup>-</sup>) acts as the nucleophile, and attacks the boron.
	2. The boron is negatively-charged and the C-B bond is broken to remove the hydroxy group, forming C-O-B bonds.
	3. The hydroxide attacks the boron again, and a negative charge is pushed onto the hydrogen.
	4. The proton quenches the negative charge.
