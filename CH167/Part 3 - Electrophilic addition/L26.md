## 2. Radical reactions of alkenes
- We can use borane, hydrogen peroxide, and sodium hydroxide to make anti-Markovnikov alcohol.
- We can use radicals to make the anti-Markovnikov product for HBr.
- When the HBr was added to an alkene in oxygen with peroxides (R-O-O-R), the addition was done anti-Markovnikov-ly.
### 2.1. Peroxides
- Peroxides can undergo homolytic bond cleavage quite easily.
- Consider dibenzoyl peroxide, PhCOO-OOCPh.
	- Their O-O bond only has a bond enthalpy of 139 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.
	- So with some heat or light they can break homolytically.
	- This is a **homolytic fission**.
- Their homolytic fission generates two benzoyl radicals.
### 2.2. Structure and stability of radicals
#### 2.2.1 Structure of radicals
- We know that:
	- Carbocations are sp<sup>2</sup>, planar
	- Carbanions are sp<sup>3</sup>, tetrahedral.
- For radicals, they are neither. 
	- It forms a structure in between planar and tetrahedral.
#### 2.2.2. Stability of radicals
- A more substituted radical is more stable, owing to hyperconjugation.
- Resonance can also stabilise the radical by moving the radical electron.
	- The more stable a radical is, the lower its bon dissociation energy.
### 2.3. Radical chain mechanism of anti-Markovnikov addition
#### 2.3.1. Initiation
- The first step is initiation, where we generate the first radicals.
	- If we were to used hydrogen and bromine radicals, we would require 336 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> of energy, meaning that it would need to be heated to ~200 degrees for homolytic fission.
	- If we used peroxides instead, their O-O bond dissociated at only ~60 degrees.
- The R-O radical then does proton abstraction, where it takes a proton from HBr to give an alcohol.
	- This yields a Br radical.
#### 2.3.2. Propagation
- The bromine radical forms a bond with one of the π-electrons in the alkene C=C.
	- The other electron is pushed onto the adjacent carbon, forming a new radical.
- This radical abstracts a proton from another molecule of HBr, so bromoalkane forms and a Br radical is regenerated.
#### 2.3.3. Termination
- There are side reactions that can occur.
	- Two Br radicals formin a bromine
	- A bromoalkane radical reacting with a bromine to give dibromoalkane
	- Two bromoalkane radicals reacting to dimerise.
- These are unlikely due to the low concentration of radicals.
### 2.4. Halogenating alkanes
- The process is exactly the same, with the exception of initiation involving homolytic cleavage of X-X bonds between halogens.
	- They are weak enough to undergo homolytic fission.
- During propagation a halogen radical abstracts a proton from the alkane to yield an alkane radical and HX.
	- This radical then reacts with a X<sub>2</sub> molecule of halogen to give the haloalkane, regenerating the halogen radical.
- During termination:
	- Two halogen radicals form a halogen molecule
	- A alkane radical and a halogen radical forms the haloalkane (yay!)
	- Alkane radicals dimerise
