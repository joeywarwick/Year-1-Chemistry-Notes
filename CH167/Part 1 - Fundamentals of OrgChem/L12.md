## 2. Elimination reactions
- Elimination reactions can occur at the same time as substitution reactions.
- There are three main types.
	- One step bimolecular reaction - E2
	- Two step unimolecular reaction - E1
		- Carbanion intermediate - E1<sub>CB</sub>
		- Carbocation intermediate - E1
### 2.1. E2
- The base attacks a hydrogen on the α-carbon, forming a double bond between the α- and β-carbon.
	- α is the carbon the group concerned is attached to
	- β is the carbon(s) adjacent to the α.
- This reaction has one TS.
- E2 tends to form E-isomers, because the anti-periplanar conformer is preferred.
#### 2.1.1. Competition of E2 and S<sub>N</sub>2
- E2 is preferred over S<sub>N</sub>2 where:
	- More basic nucleophile/bases are used.
	- Nucleophiles are more sterically hindered
	- Reaction temperatures are higher.
		- This is because they form three molecules.
			- So positive entropy change
		- So they are more favourable at higher temps.
### 2.2. E1
- The C-X bond is broken first, creating a carbocation on the α-carbon.
- A base then picks up an hydrogen on the β-carbon.
- So a C=C bond forms between the two carbons.
	- The carbocation formation is the RDS.
#### 2.2.1. Competition between E1 and S<sub>N</sub>1
- E1 is preferred over S<sub>N</sub>1 where:
	- Temperature is higher
		- For similar reasons to E2. Positive entropy change
	- Weak, non-nucleophilic bases
		- e.g. HSO<sub>4</sub><sup>-</sup>.
