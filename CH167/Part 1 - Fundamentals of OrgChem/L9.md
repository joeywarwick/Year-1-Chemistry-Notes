## 2. Selectivity (cont.)
- We also need to choose the best electrophile.
### 2.1. Electron deficiency
- Consider the following five carbonyl compounds:
	- MeCOCl
	- MeCHO
	- MeCOMe
	- MeCOOMe
	- MeCONHMe
#### 2.1.1. Inductive effect
- We can see that MeCOCl is more electrophilic than MeCHO, which is more electrophilic than MeCOMe.
	- This is because Cl is EWG, while Me is EDG.
	- So the carbonyl in MeCOCl will have a larger partial positive charge.
#### 2.1.2. Mesomeric effect
- From MeCOMe to MeCONHMe the electrophilicity continues to decrease.
	- This is because there is +M into the carbonyl carbon, which makes the partial positive charge very small.
#### 2.1.3. Steric effects
- Consider a epoxide, MeC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>3</sub>O.
	- Nucleophiles would attack from the right.
		- This is because the left side is sterically hindered by the methyl group, so Nu prefer the right.
	- Attacking from different sides yield two **regioisomers**, which are structural isomers.
> **NOTE** In this example we can also reason using the inductive effect. Me is EDG, so will lower electrophilicity of the LHS regardless.
### 2.3. Final destination of electrons
- We can consider the suitability of the final destination using a range of parameters too.
#### 2.3.1. Energetics of intermediates
- Consider propene reacting with HBr.
	- Depending on whether the central carbon or end carbon is used, the energy of the carbocation intermediate is different.
	- If attacked with central carbon:
		- EtCH<sub>2</sub><sup>+</sup>
		- Primary carbocation.
	- If attacked with end carbon:
		- MeCH<sup>+</sup>Me
		- Secondary carbocation.
	- As a result, attacking with the end carbon is more preferable than attacking with the central carbon.
- This exhibits regioselectivity too.
#### 2.3.2. Stability of anion
- We can determine the leaving group using the pK<sub>a</sub> when there are multiple to choose from.
	- We can determine strength of base from conjugate acid.
	- Stronger acid = weaker base
	- Weaker base = better leaving group.
- Consider the leaving groups Cl<sup>-</sup>, MeO<sup>-</sup>, and EtNH<sup>-</sup>.
	- HCl has the lowest pK<sub>a</sub>, so Cl<sup>-</sup> is the best leaving group.
