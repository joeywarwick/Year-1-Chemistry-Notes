## 2. Chirality
- Objects are chiral is they have non-superimposable mirror images.
	- The opposite is **achiral**.
- Consider bromochlorofluoromethane, CFClBrH.
	- It forms non-superimposable mirror images known as **enantiomers**
	- We can see that all molecules with a tetrahedral carbon attached to four different groups are chiral.
	- The carbon is known as a **stereogenic centre**/**stereocentre**/**chiral centre**.
> All molecules with a reflection symmetry element are achiral.
- Different enantiomers can have different applications.
### 2.1. Optical rotation
- Enantiomers have identical physical and chemical properties but rotate plane polarised light in **equal** and **opposite** directions.
	- Hence chiral compounds are **optically active**.
	- Achiral compounds are therefore **optically inactive**.
- The rotation can be measured with a polarimeter.
	- Light source is polarised in a filter.
	- The polarised light is then shot through a sample.
	- The resulting light is analysed in a receiving filter.
#### 2.1.1. Racemic mixtures
- An equimolar (1:1 mole ratio) mixture of enantiomers is known as a **racemic mixture.**
	- The rotations cancel out, so there is no net rotation of light.
> Enzymes are quite good at providing enantiopure products.
## 2.2. Assigning absolute configuration
- We assign absolute rotation using the Cahn-Ingold-Prelog (CIP) rules.
	1. Assign priorities from highest atomic number to lowest
	2. Place the lowest priority group at the back
	3. If 1-2-3 is clockwise then R, if anticlockwise then S.
#### 2.2.1. Rotating the molecule
- We will need to manipulate the molecule such that the lowest priority group is at the back (unless already done).
- There are three main methods to this:
	1. Rotating in head
		- Tricky
		- Risky
		- Prone to mistakes
	2. 'Flip' method
		- Leave the lowest priority at the front and assign accordingly.
		- The final answer is the opposite of the 'apparent' answer.
	3. 'Swap' method
		- This is very useful if priority 4 (P4) is in the plane of paper.
		- First swap P4 with the group at the back to give the enantiomer.
		- Then swap any two front groups.
		- Then assign the configuration.
#### 2.2.2. Historical nomenclature
- Enantiomers are also labelled with +/-, D/L, and R/S.
	- +/-:
		- + enantiomers rotate light clockwise
		- - enantiomers rotate light anticlockwise
	- D/L:
		- Done via degradation to a known compound to assign absolute configuration.
		- Gives no information on the original compound.
> **NOTE** They cannot be assigned by 'looking' at the structures.
