## 2. Shapes of molecules
- s- and p-orbitals can hybridise to give HAOs (See CH164).
	- If 3 p- and 1s-orbitals mix it becomes a sp<sup>3</sup> orbital.
		- It has 75% p-character and 25% scharacter
	- If 2 p- and 1s-orbitals mix it becomes a sp<sup>2</sup> orbital.
		- It has 67% p-character and 33% s-character
	- If 1 p- and 1s-orbitals mix it becomes a sp<sup></sup> orbital.
		- It has 50% of each orbital's character.
- Note that sp<sup>3</sup> has higher energy than sp<sup>2</sup> which has higher energy than sp.
- We can determine the hybridisation very easily:

| BP + LP | sp<sup>x</sup> |
| ------- | -------------- |
| 4       | 3              |
| 3       | 2              |
| 2       | 1              |
- Exceptions are on atoms with lone pairs that are next to π-bonds.
	- If they are sp<sup>2</sup> hybridised the LP can be in the p-orbital, so can form conjugated system with the π-orbitals.
### 2.1. Rotational barriers
- For alkanes, rotating the C-C bonds have no effect on the bonding.
	- C-C bonds can undergo rotation easily.
		- Rotation barrier ~12 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
- For alkenes, rotating around the C=C bond break the overlap between the p-orbitals.
	- Ethene does not undergo rotations.
		- Rotation barrier ~260 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
> Any rotational barrier over 105 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup> will not rotate.
#### 2.1.1. Conformations
- Alkane's rotation barrier comes from the conformers that it exists in.
- Consider ethane. It has two methyl groups connected together with a C-C bond.
	- Because of electron-electron repulsion, the hydrogens mildly repel the hydrogens on the other methyl group.
	- The lowest-energy conformer is the **staggered** conformer, where the hydrogens are between the hydrogens on the opposite side.
		- This maximises distance between hydrogens.
	- The highest energy conformer is the **eclipsed** conformer, where the hydrogens are 'overlapping' ('eclipsed').
		- This minimises distance between isomers.
- The conformers are represented using the Newman projection.
- We use **dihedral** angle to define a conformer.
	- 0 degrees is eclipsed
	- 60 degrees is staggered
	- 120 degrees is eclipsed
	- ...
- So if we plotted a graph of energy against dihedral angle, it would show a **sine curve**.
- There are more complex conformers for butane.
	- As it has four carbons with hydrogens bonded to it, it has four different conformers.
		- syn-periplanar, 0
			- highest energy, all on one side
		- gauche, 60
			- Staggered around terminal C-C, eclipsed at centre.
		- anticlinal, 120
			- Staggered conformation of the central carbons, eclipsed around the end C-C bonds
		- anti-periplanar, 180
			- Staggered all round, terminal Me on opposite sides
