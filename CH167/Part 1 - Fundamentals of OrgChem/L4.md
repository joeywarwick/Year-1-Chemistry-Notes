## 2. pK<sub>a</sub>
- An alternate evidence for sp carbanions being more stable is the pK<sub>a</sub> of their conjugate acids.
	- Methane (sp<sup>3</sup>) is 50
	- Benzene (sp<sup>2</sup>) is 45
	- Ethyne (sp) is 25.
- Recall from CH165 pK<sub>a</sub> is the ability of a compound to dissociate to donate a proton.
	- The more capable a molecule is the lower the pK<sub>a</sub>.
		- So the conjugate base is more stable.
### 2.1. pK<sub>a</sub> and pH
- pK<sub>a</sub> is pH when half of the acid dissociates.
- For example, consider acetic (ethanoic acid).
	- pK<sub>a</sub> = 4.76, so at pH = 4.76:
	- Half of the acid exists in molecular form
	- Half of the acid exists in dissociated form.
- So at pH below the pK<sub>a</sub> the acid is largely in undissociated form.
	- Vice versa
- From this we can then say that:
	- Weak acids have strong bases (difficult to stabilise)
	- Strong acids have weak bases (easy to stabilise)
#### 2.1.1. Mixing acids
- Consider a mixture of acetate (CH<sub>3</sub>COO<sup>-</sup>) and phenoxide (C<sub>6</sub>H<sub>5</sub>O<sup>-</sup>)
	- Acetic acid pK<sub>a</sub> 4.76
	- Phenol pK<sub>a</sub> 10
		- So protons will prioritise protonating the phenoxide as it is a stronger base.
- Difference in pK<sub>a</sub> gives the logarithm of the equilibrium constant:
	- $CH_3COOH + C_6H_5O^- \rightleftharpoons CH_3COO^- + C_6H_5OH$
		- $10 - 4.76 = 5.24 = \log{K}$
		- $K \approx 174000$
			- So for every molecule of acetic acid there will be 174000 acetate
			- For every molecule of phenoxide there will be 1740000 phenol.
### 2.2 Factors affecting pK<sub>a</sub>
- The degree of dissociation is affected by three main factors:
	- Stability of conjugate base
		- Electronegativity of atom
		- Hybridisation
		- Size
		- Delocalisation
		- Inductive effect
	- Strength of H-A bond
	- Solvent
#### 2.2.1. Electronegativity
- HF is stronger than HCH<sub>3</sub>.
	- This is because F is very electronegative.
		- Higher Z<sub>eff</sub>
		- Anion closer to nucleus
#### 2.2.2. Delocalisation
- HCCH is stronger than HC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>
	- C-C triple bond delocalisation stabilises the anion
#### 2.2.3. Size
- HI is stronger than HF.
	- Larger anions spread the charge out more
	- So more stable
> Same period - electronegativity dominates
> Same group - size dominates
#### 2.2.4. Hybridisation
- HCCH is stronger than HC<sub>2</sub>H<sub>5</sub>
	- sp lower energy than sp<sup>3</sup>
	- So anion more stable
#### 2.2.5. Inductive effect
- CH<sub>4</sub> stronger than CH<sub>3</sub>CH(CH<sub>3</sub>)CH<sub>3</sub>
	- Tertiary carbanions more destabilised than methyl carbanion due to alkyl groups' EDG character.
> Mesomeric effects more significant than inductive effects

### 2.3. Solvent
- Stabilisation of the anion/proton through solvation affects pK<sub>a</sub> greatly.
- We categorise solvents into three categories:
	- Polar protic
		- Can stabilise cations (LP/dipole)
		- Can stabilise anions (H-bonding)
		- e.g.
			- Water
			- Formic acid
	- Polar aprotic
		- Can stabilise cations (LP/dipole)
		- e.g.
			- DMSO
			- Ethyl acetate
			- Acetonitrile
			- Acetone
	- Apolar
		- No stabilisation
		- e.g.
			- Hexane
			- Toluene
- Different categories result in different pK<sub>a</sub>.
	- Consider methanol
		- pK<sub>a</sub> in water = 16
		- pK<sub>a</sub> in DMSO = 29
### 2.4. pK<sub>a</sub> of nitrogen compounds
- Amines are weak acids.
	- It dissociates into a proton and an amide.
> **NOTE** Amides are nitrogens with a negative charge. It is different from amine functional group.
- Instead, they are more commonly used as bases.
	- $R_2N + H^+ \rightleftharpoons R_2NH^+$
	- While we do have pK<sub>b</sub>, it is not used to assess the basicity of a molecule.
	- Instead we use the pK<sub>a</sub> of the conjugate acid.
		- This is also known as using the pK<sub>a</sub><sup>H</sup>.
#### 2.4.1. pK<sub>a</sub><sup>H</sup> of amines
- Two main factors determine the pK<sub>a</sub><sup>H</sup> of amines:
	- 'Availability' of LP
	- Stability of the ammonium cation formed.
- Regarding the 'availability of LP':
	- sp is **less basic** than sp<sup>3</sup>.
		- This is because with increasing s-character the electrons are held closer to the nucleus.
		- So it is harder to accept a proton.
	- Delocalisation decreases the availability of the LP, so conjugated amine are less basic.
- Regarding stability of ammonium cations:
	- Delocalisation of the ammonium cations increases basicity.
		- The positive charge on the cation is spread out and stabilised
> More resonance forms of ammonium cation = more stable = more basic.
