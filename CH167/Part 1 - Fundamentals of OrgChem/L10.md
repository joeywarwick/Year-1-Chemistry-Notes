## 2. Substitution at saturated carbon
- Consider a reaction.
	- $R-CH_2-X + Nu^- \rightarrow R-CH_2-Nu + X^-$
- This reaction can progress by two pathways:
	- S<sub>N</sub>1, which occurs in two steps via a carbocation intermediate
	- S<sub>N</sub>2, which occurs in a one-step reaction to 'directly' form the product.
### 2.1. S<sub>N</sub>2
- S<sub>N</sub>2 reaction's name comes from:
	- S - substitution
	- N - nucleophilic
	- 2 - bimolecular
- As the name suggests, it's first step involves nucleophilic attack from the nucleophile.
	- This forms a negatively-charged intermediate.
		- This is because both the X and the Nu have partial negative charges.
	- In the intermediate, we use dotted lines to represent the Nu--C bond and the C--X bond.
		- They are not fully bonding, one bond is about to form and one is breaking.
#### 2.1.1. Energetics of S<sub>N</sub>2 reactions
- As it is a single-step reaction that does not go via any intermediates, the reaction profile will have one transition state only.
> It's exothermic.
#### 2.1.2. Kinetics of S<sub>N</sub>2 reactions
- As there are two reactants in the first and only step, the rate equation is
	- $Rate = k[Nu^-][RX]$
#### 2.1.3. Other factors affecting rate
- The rate is also dependent on the nucleophilicity of the nucleophile.
	- Higher nucleophilicity, higher rate.
		-  Consider the factors determining nucleophilicity (See L8)
			- Steric
			- Electronegativity
> **NOTE** For a **single element** nucleophilicity is correlated with the basicity.
- Like the nucleophile, the rate is also dependent on the ability of X to act as a leaving group.
	- Less basic groups are better leaving groups.
	- Weaker C-X bonds give better leaving groups.
> Similar trends for S<sub>N</sub>1.
- The carbon itself can also affect the rate.
	- Methyl > Primary > Secondary > Tertiary.
		- This is because alkyl groups are EDGs. They reduce the partial positive charge on carbon.
		- Another reason is the steric hindrance as a result of the alkyl groups' presence.
	- Carbons with EWGs (phenyl, hydroxy) attached are faster. 
		- The EWGs increase the size of the partial positive charge on the carbon.
	- Carbons with adjacent π-systems are better. 
		- The π-system stabilises the transition state
> **NOTE** S<sub>N</sub>2 at sp<sup>2</sup> carbons (e.g. benzene) is not possible. 
	- The π-electrons
- Sometimes we can alter compounds to provide better leaving groups.
	- Consider an alcohol.
		- Hydroxide ions are poor leaving groups
		- So protonating the oxygen gives water, which is a good leaving group.
### 2.2. Stereochemistry of S<sub>N</sub>2 reactions
- The Nu attacks from 'behind' the leaving group.
	- So the leaving group is 'pushed out'.
- If the electrophile is chiral, S<sub>N</sub>2 **inverses** the stereochemical configuration.
	- R to S etc.
