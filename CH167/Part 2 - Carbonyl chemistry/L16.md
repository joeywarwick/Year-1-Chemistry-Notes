## 2. Carboxylic acid and derivatives
- Carboxylic acid has a carbonyl group with an hydroxy group attached to it.
	- The oxygen LP can form a π-system with the π-electrons in the C=O bond. This form a conjugated system.
### 2.1. Derivatives
- Following the general structure of RCOX, X can be a range of molecules.
	- Cl (acid chloride)
	- OCOR' (acid anhydride)
	- OR' (ester)
	- NHR' (amide)
- They are all EWGs, so -I at carbon centre, larger partial positive charge.
#### 2.1.1. Reactivity of derivatives
- Consider the following derivatives and their hydrolysis to carboxylic acids.
	- Acyl chloride - 20 degrees, fast
	- Acyl anhydride - 20 degrees, slow
	- Ester - Heat, acid/base catalysis
	- Amide - High temperature, acid/base catalysis.
> **NOTE** Acyl chloride and acid chloride are synonymous.
- We can correlate this to the activation effects they experience
	- Acyl chloride
		- 2 x -I
		- Weak +M
	- Acyl anhydride
		- 2 x -I
		- Moderate +M
	- Ester
		- 2x -I
		- Moderately strong +M
	- Amide
		- 2x -I
		- Strong +M
- Another factor is the leaving group.
	- Overall there are three factors:

| Factor            |
| ----------------- |
| Stability of LG X |
| C-X bond strength |
| Solvent effects   |
### 2.2. Synthesis of carboxylic acid and derivatives
- Carboxylic acids are synthesised using Grignard and carbon dioxide.
- After the carboxylic acid is synthesised we might need to convert it to it's derivatives for better reactivity.
#### 2.2.1. Acid chlorides
- We synthesise acid chlorides by reacting the corresponding carboxylic acid with thionyl chloride.
	- $RCOOH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow ROCl + SO_2 + HCl$
- The reaction mechanism is as illustrated below:
	1. Hydroxy group on the carbon forms double bond with carbon (C=OH)
	2. The π-electrons in the carbonyl oxygen C=O is used to attack the sulfur on SOCl<sub>2</sub>.
	3. One of the chlorines is ejected as the leaving group.
	4. The chloride anion formed attacks the carbonyl carbon
	5. The other S-Cl bond breaks and the chloride attacks the hydrogen, forming HCl
		- At the same time the O-H bond breaks, and a C=O bond forms.
	6. The C-O bond breaks, forming S=O.
> **NOTE** This is the 'concerted addition mechanism' and it is not entirely correct.
- As SO<sub>2</sub> and HCl are gases, there is a large entropy gain that makes this reaction thermodynamically favourable.
