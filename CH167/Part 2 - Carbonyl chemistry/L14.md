## 2. Nucleophilic addition to carbonyls
- There are two mechanisms for this. The first:
	- Nucleophilic attack of the carbonyl carbon yields a tetrahedral intermediate.
		- This intermediate has a negative charge as a result of heterolytic breaking of the C=O π-bond.
	- This intermediate is quenched by a proton.
- The second:
	- The carbonyl oxygen is protonated, causing the π-bond to break heterolytically, forming a carbocation intermediate.
	- The nucleophile reacts with the carbocation to yield a final product.
### 2.1. Reactivity of carbonyl compounds
- Consider four carbonyls:
	- Formaldehyde, HCHO
	- Acetaldehyde, MeCHO
	- Acetone, MeCOMe
	- Methyl vinyl ketone, MeCOCHCH<sub>2</sub>.
- There reactivity is dependent on the stabilisation of the carbocation.
	- Formaldehyde experiences no stabilisation, because it has hydrogen has its R-groups.
	- Acetaldehyde experiences some stabilisation as the result of methyl group's EDG effect (+I)
	- Acetone receives even more stabilisation from the two methyl groups (2 +!)
	- Methyl vinyl ketone experiences 2 +I and resonance stabilisation from the conjugate system between the carbonyl π-electrons and the C=C π-electrons.
- In addition, all of the above carbonyls except formaldehyde are also stabilised by hyperconjugation.
### 2.2. Addition of cyanide ions
- It is also an acid catalysed reaction in aqueous solution.
	- $RCOR+CN^- \rightarrow RCOH(CN)R$
- These reaction are reversible.
	- Reacting with a more basic nucleophile e.g. OH<sup>-</sup> reverses the reaction.
#### 2.2.1 Steric effects on the position of equilibrium
- The carbonyl groups have a sp<sup>2</sup> carbon, meaning the the groups are 120 degrees from each other.
- The reaction forms a tetrahedral intermediate, meaning that the groups are now only 109.5 degrees apart.
	- So there is steric crowding.
- The less steric crowing the greater yield of products.
### 2.3. Addition of organolithium compounds
- Organolithium compounds are a way to form new C-C bonds.
- Consider methyllithium.
	- They have a very highly polarised C-Li bond, so polarised that it is resembling an ionic compound.
- With carbonyl compounds the carbon attacks like a nucleophile, forming a <sup>(-)</sup>C-CR<sub>2</sub>O carbanion, which the lithium cation is bound to.
	- The lithium cation is removed and the carbanion quenched with acid.
> This reaction is done in anhydrous solvents like THF or Diethyl ether. This is because organolithium compounds are very reactive with water.
	- $MeLi + H_2O \rightarrow CH_4 + LiOH$
	- This reaction releases large quantities of heat.
### 2.4. Grignard reagents
- Grignard reagents are organomagnesium compounds with formula RMgX. 
	- It is made by adding magnesium to a halo(typically bromo)alkane in diethyl ether.
	- The C-Mg bond is very polarised, making the carbon capable of acting like a nucleophile.
