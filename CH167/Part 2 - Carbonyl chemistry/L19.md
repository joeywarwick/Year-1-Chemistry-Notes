## 2. Formation and stability of enols
- Carboxylic acids can under go **tautomerisation**.
	- By breaking the O-H bond to from the C=O bond then using the other C=O bond to pick up another proton, the location of C=O and O-H bonds can exchange very quickly.
	- They are **tautomers** and they exist in equilibrium.
> **NOTE** This is not resonance, chemical structure has changed. Tautomers are structural isomers whose structure depend on a movable proton.
- We know this exists from labelling studies.
	- We know this is the case from labelling studies
		- We mark an oxygen with O-18.
- The equilibrium constant is ~1
- We also know this occurs in some other compounds, e.g. diketones from proton NMR.
### 2.1. Keto-enol tautomerism
- Like stated above, ketones can form enols, which are tautomers of each other.
- Consider acetone.
	- $CH_3COCH_3 \rightleftharpoons CH_3OH=CH_2$
	- The LHS is the keto form and the RHS is the enol form.
> **NOTE** We should be using asymmetrical equilibrium arrows where the rate constant is not 1.
- The equilibrium constant is about 10<sup>-6</sup>, so is a lot to the LHS.
- Consider the bonds present in the molecules
	- C=O and C-H on LHS total 1160 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>
	- C=C and O-H on RHS total 1120 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.
	- This difference is energy is the reason behind the low K.
- In order for a enol to be able to form, a carbon is required adjacent to the carbonyl carbon. 
	- This carbon is the α-carbon, so we can say an alpha carbon is required.
- Furthermore, an α-proton is required.
- As enols have a C=C bond, we can assign E/Z isomerism to them.
#### 2.1.1. Mechanisms of enol formation -acid
1. Protonation of carbonyl oxygen
2. Removal of hydrogen with another water, electrons used to from C=C, C=OH break to C-OH.
#### 2.1.2. Mechanisms of enol formation - base
1. Hydroxide attacks hydrogen, pushes negative charge onto carbonyl oxygen.
2. Oxygen takes a proton from water
#### 2.1.3. Enol stability
- As seen above, if the enol is not stabilised it will favour turning back to the ketone.
- It can be stabilised via:
	- Conjugation of the C=C bond with another system.
	- Intramolecular hydrogen bonding between the hydroxyl group and another heteroatom.
	- Enols provide aromaticity.
		- Consider a cyclohexadienone.
		- Enolisation converts to phenol.
		- This is so good it almost exists exclusively as phenol.
