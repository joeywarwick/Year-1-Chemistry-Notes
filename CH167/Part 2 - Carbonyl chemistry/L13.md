## 2. The carbonyl group
- Many functional groups contain carbonyl(s):
	- Aldehyde (R-CHO)
	- Ketone (R-CO-R)
	- Carboxylic acid (R-COOH)
	- Acid anhydride (R-CO-O-CO-R)
	- Ester (R-COO-R)
	- Amide (RCONR<sub>2</sub>)
	- Thioester (R-COSR)
### 2.1. Structure and bonding
- Consider formaldehyde, HCHO.
	- Both the carbon and oxygen are sp<sup>2</sup>.
	- The carbon is therefore planar.
## 3. Reduction of alcohols
- Alcohols can be oxidised to ketones and aldehydes.
- Different degrees of substitution react differently.
- All involves an oxidation agent:
	- e.g. CrO<sub>3</sub>
- All occur in acidic conditions in aqueous solution.
### 3.1. Primary alcohols
- Primary alcohols under an oxidation agent and acidic conditions are oxidised in two steps.
	- First to aldehydes, then to carboxylic acids.
- To stop at the aldehyde we require gentler oxidising agents.
	- So we use PCC in **anhydrous conditions**.
> The water in carboxylic acid oxidation plays an important role by forming hydrides. Reacting in anhydrous conditions prevent this.
### 3.2. Secondary alcohols
- Secondary alcohols are oxidised to ketones.
### 3.3. Tertiary alcohols
- No hydrogens are available for removal.
- So **no oxidation**.
### 3.4. Monitoring reaction with IR
- There are substantial changes as the reaction progresses.
- The O-H vibrations disappear and is replaced with C=O vibrations.
