## 2. The Aldol reaction
- Consider acetone, MeCOMe.
	- If reacted with sodium methoxide, NaOMe at **RT**:
		- A self-reaction occurs.
		- This forms the 'aldol product', hydroxyketone
		- This reaction proceeds as following
			1. Deprotonation with methoxide to enol
			2. Attacks another acetone
			3. Forms the aldol product
		- A ketone or aldehyde with a mild base results in a self reaction, known as an **Aldol reaction**.
	- If reacted with NaOMe with heat, or NaOMe then quenching with acid:
		- Another self-reaction occurs.
		- This forms an enone.
> **NOTE** The enone is essentially the hydroxyketone following a condensation reaction to remove a molecule of water.
- The aldol reaction to hydroxyketone is simple and analogous to a nucleophilic addtion.
	- It is done in methanol so the final negative charge can be quenched and methoxide regenerated.
### 2.1. Enone synthesis
- The synthesis of the enone starts after the synthesis of the hydroxyketone.
	1. The central carbon is deprotonated with MeO to push negative charge onto the carbonyl oxygen, forming a C=C bond.
	2. The negative charge then passes down and ejects the -OH.
- This is alternatively done, as stated above, using acid catalyst.
	- The mechanism is largely unchanged with the exception of protonating the hydroxy group first.
		1. Hydroxy group protonated
		2. Hydroxy group ejected as water, forming carbocation
		3. Central carbon deprotonated, C=C forms, proton regenerated.
### 2.2. Intramolecular aldol
- The aldol reaction can also occur within the same molecule.
	- In diones, if one is enolised, the it can attack the other carbonyl group.
	- This yield a cyclic hydroxyketone.
- **NOTE** The enol can attack another molecule to from a side product, but **intramolecular reactions are always faster**.
### 2.3. Crossed aldol
- Crossed = two different carbonyls
- When we react with two different species of carbonyls, we are presented with problems:
	- A self-reaction between molecules of A
	- B self-reaction between molecules of B
	- Unwanted site of enolisation leads to side products\
#### 2.3.1. Controlling aldols
- We have some solutions and methods to control crossed aldols:
	- Only use one species capable of enolisation.
	- Ensure the enolising molecule is **more electrophilic**.
		- So attack cannot occur in the opposite direction.
> Remember that formaldehyde is the most electrophilic, and amides are not very electrophilic.
## 3. The Claisen reaction
- We apply a similar logic to esters.
	- Esters in a mild base yields sell-reactions.
- Consider reaction of methyl acetate, MeCOOMe.
	1. After enolisation of methyl acetate, it attacks another molecule of methyl acetate.
	2. This gives a favourable OMe leaving group.
	3. eprotonation occurs again at the central oxygen, forming a C=C bond and a negative charge on the ester oxygen.
	4.  This is worked-up with acid to quench the reaction.
- Similar to aldols, it can also have intramolecular reactions to give cyclic ketoesters.
