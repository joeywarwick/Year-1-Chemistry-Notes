## 2. Magnetic moments
- The magnetic moment, μ, is related to total angular momentum, J in the following relationship:
	- $\mu^2 \propto J(J+1)$ 
- J is in turn a sum of:
	- Spin, S; and
	- Angular momentum, L.
- J = S + L
> For 3d TMs we can simplify J to be proportional to the spin, as the orbital part is mostly quenched.
### 2.1. Calculating magnetic moments
- Based on this, we can calculate μ in two ways:
	- $\mu = 2\sqrt{S(S+1)}\mu_B$ 
		- μ<sub>B</sub> is **Bohr magnetons**, the unit.
		- S is **total spin**.
			- Each unpaired electrons **carry a spin of 0.5**, therefore:
				- $S = \frac{1}{2}n$
	- $\mu = \sqrt{n(n+2)}\mu_B$ 
		- This is obtained by substituting 1/2 n into the formula.
- There are minor deviations between calculated and experimental, but broadly speaking, the model is valid.
## 3. Crystal Field Stabilisation Energy (CFSE)
- CFSE, also known as Ligand Field Stabilisation Energy (LFSE), is the additional energy that arises from the **splitting of the d-orbitals.**
### 3.1. Theoretical calculation of CFSE
- This can be calculated using Δ<sub>oct</sub>.
- Take d<sup>2</sup>:
	- It has configuration t<sub>2g</sub><sup>2</sup>.
	- We know that the energy difference between the d-orbitals and t<sub>2g</sub> is $-\frac{2}{5} \Delta_{oct}$.
	- So $CFSE = 2(-\frac{2}{5} \Delta_{oct}) = -\frac{4}{5} \Delta_{oct}$
> **NOTE** Minus is used to show that the energy has decreased.
- Take high-spin d<sup>4</sup>:
	- It has configuration t<sub>2g</sub><sup>3</sup> e<sub>g</sub><sup>1</sup>.
	- We also know that the energy difference between the d-orbitals and e<sub>g</sub> is $\frac{3}{5} \Delta_{oct}$.
	- So $CFSE = 3(-\frac{2}{5} \Delta_{oct}) + \frac{3}{5} \Delta_{oct} = -\frac{3}{5} \Delta_{oct}$ 
- Take low-spin:
	- It has configuration t<sub>2g</sub><sup>4</sup>.
	- $CFSE = 4(-\frac{2}{5} \Delta_{oct}) = -\frac{8}{5} \Delta_{oct}$
	- But there is also **pairing energy** (See L3).
- So in general, for  t<sub>2g</sub><sup>x</sup> e<sub>g</sub><sup>y</sup>:
	- $CFSE = x(-\frac{2}{5} \Delta_{oct}) + y(\frac{3}{5} \Delta_{oct}) + Pairing \space energy$ 
- C/LFSE is defined for a **free ion**. So the pairing energy excludes what is already present in the ion.
	- Take d<sup>6</sup>:
		- It already has a paired electron, so:
			- High-spin retains the same configuration, so no extra pairing energy.
			- Low-spin pairs two further electrons, so 2x pairing energy.
	- So when calculating the C/LFSE, the number of pairing energies added should be determined **in comparison to the free ion.**
> **NOTE** No pairing energy contribution for **high-spin**, as the **free ion must also be high-spin.**
### 3.2. Experimental calculation of CFSE
- There is experimental evidence for pairing energies and CFSE.
- In a plot of the hydration enthalpies of 3rd period metal 2+ ions, only d<sup>0</sup>, d<sup>5</sup>, and d<sup>10</sup> lied on an interpolated linear line.
	- If there were no CFSE, hydration enthalpy would depend entirely on charge density, which is related to the ionic size.
	- These ions land on the interpolated line because they have no stabilisation.
- Take hexaaquanickel (II), [Ni(OH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>]<sup>2+</sup>:
	- UV-Vis gives Δ<sub>oct</sub> as 8500 cm<sup>-1</sup>.
	- We cannot measure interpolated value of Ni<sup>2+</sub>'s hydration enthalpy, because it does not exist.
	- We can, however, subtract Mn<sup>2+</sup> from Zn<sup>2+</sup>, then multiply by 3/5.
		- This yields 2859 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.
	- The difference between the expected and actual value is 127 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.
	- We know CFSE = -6/5 Δ<sub>oct</sub>, so we can calculate Δ<sub>oct</sub> to be 106 kJ mol<sup>-1</sup>.
		- = 8861 cm<sup>-1</sup>, which is close to measured value from UV-Vis.
### 3.3. Effects of CFSE
- CFSE does not determine the thermodynamic stability of the complex - it dictates the kinetics.
	- Lower CFSE leads to slower reactions because of more stabilisation.
- Take hexaaquanickel (II), [Ni(OH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>]<sup>2+</sup> again:
	- It will readily react with CN<sup>-</sup> to from [Ni(CN)<sub>6</sub>]<sup>4-</sup>, although the reaction may not be particularly fast.
- We therefore do not say **stable** or **unstable**.
	- Instead we describe complexes as **inert** or **labile**.
> If a complex is so inert heating the system has limited effect, it can be excited with UV light to a higher spin state, reducing the CFSE.
