## 2. Tetrahedral complexes
- Tetrahedral complexes have coordination number 4.
- In a cube, the ligands will be at the opposite corners.
- While we still have the d-orbitals they are no longer grouped into t<sub>2g</sub> and e<sub>g</sub>. 
	- They are instead t<sub>2</sub> and e, without the g for gerader.
### 2.1. Orbital splitting in tetrahedral complexes.
- They still split, but due to the positioning of the ligands, now t<sub>2</sub> is pointing at the ligands.
	- So t<sub>2</sub> will be higher than e.

| e, lower energy                         | t<sub>2</sub>, higher energy |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| d<sub>x<sup>2</sup>-y<sup>2</sup></sub> | d<sub>xy</sub>               |
| d<sub>z<sup>2</sup></sub>               | d<sub>xz</sub>               |
|                                         | d<sub>yz</sub>               |
- Similarly, instead of Δ<sub>oct</sub> it is now Δ<sub>td</sub>, 'delta tetrahedral'.
	- For the same metal and ligands Δ<sub>oct</sub> is **always larger** than Δ<sub>td</sub>. This is due to two reasons:
		- Only having four ligands, so effect diminished.
		- No direct pointing, of ligands at orbitals - a bit more nuance.
	-  We can quantify  Δ<sub>td</sub>. $\Delta_{td} = \frac{4}{9}\Delta_{oct}$ 
-  The size of Δ<sub>td</sub> means that tetrahedral complexes are **always high-spin**.
## 3. Jahn-Teller Effect
> **NOTE** The Jahn-Teller effect originated from the Jahn-Teller theorem: '**All non-linear nuclear configurations are unstable for an orbitally degenerate state'**. But remembering this is **not particularly meaningful.**
- **Jahn-Teller effect** (J-T effect) is when **orbitally degenerate non-linear molecules** distort to **avoid the degeneracy** and **achieve lower energies**.
- Take a d<sup>4</sup>, e.g. Cr<sup>2+</sup>.
	- If we hydrated it, we form a hexaaquacomplex, Hexaaquachromium (II). ([Cr(OH<sub>2</sub>)<sub>6</sub>]<sup>2+</sup>)
	- Because water is a weak-field ligand, the chromium will be **high-spin**.
		- So electronic configuration t<sub>2g</sub><sup>3</sup> e<sub>g</sub><sup>1</sup>.
	- The octahedron will further stabilise itself by stretching the axial bonds.
		- So the two ligands bonded in the axial position will be further away.
	- This lowers the energy of the d<sub>z<sup>2</sup></sub> orbitals.
	- So the e<sub>g</sub> orbitals are no longer degenerate.
	- The opposite happens for the equatorial ligands, where the bonds shorten
		- This increases the energy of d<sub>xy</sub> slightly, and decreases the energy for the other two.
	- So we lose even more degeneracy.
		- We can see that we went from one energy level in a free ion to four with the Jahn-Teller effect.

		- There are no net energy changes for the t<sub>2g</sub> orbitals.

### 3.1. Determining distortion
> **NOTE** We cannot tell how the complex will distort if it has six identical ligands.
- For trans-complexes with two types of ligands in 4:2 ratio:
	- If the two ligands were stronger-field ligands, they will want to make stronger bonds.
		- So the equatorial ligands will squash.
	- If the two ligands were weaker-field ligands, they will be prome to squashing
		- So the equatorial ligands will stretch.
- d<sup>1</sup> will always be **squashed**, because the doubly-stabilised d<sub>xy</sub> provides more stabilisation than stretched.
- d<sup>2</sup> will always be **stretched**, because the destabilised orbitals will reduce overall stabilising effect.
- d<sup>3</sup>, d<sup>6</sup> LS results in a perfect octahedron, because all orbitals are evenly occupied.
> The t<sub>2g</sub> electrons determine whether distortion occurs. The e<sub>g</sub> electrons determine the degree of distortion.
### 3.2. Extreme effects of Jahn-Teller
- If the two axial ligands are pushed so far away the bonds are broken, the complex becomes a D<sub>4h</sub> square planar complex with coordination number 4.
	- So we can view **square planar complexes** as a **octahedral complex with extreme distortion.**
		- d<sub>z<sup>2</sup></sub> is **stabilised greatly**;
		- d<sub>x<sup>2</sup>-y<sup>2</sup></sub> **increases in energy slightly.**
- d<sup>3</sup> in particular is greatly stabilised in square planar complexes.
- Same case for d<sup>4</sup>, d<sup>5</sup> LS, d<sup>6</sup> LS, and d<sup>8</sup>.
	- As a result, Ni<sup>2+</sup>, Pd<sup>2+</sup>, and Pt<sup>2+</sup> are the primary ions that will form square planar complexes.
		- This effect is enhanced by strong-field ligands.
