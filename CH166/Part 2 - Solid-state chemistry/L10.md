### 2.1. Density
- We can calculate the density of a metal given its unit cell size and packing method.
- Consider copper:
	- Properties:
		- M<sub>r</sub> = 0.063546 kg mol<sup>-1</sup>
		- Unit cell = FCC, a = 3.61 A
	- $d = \frac{m}{v}$
	- 4 atoms per unit cell, unit cells in a mole $\frac{N_A}{4} = 1.5055 \times 10^{23}$
	- Mass of one unit cell = $63.546 \div 1.5055 \times 10^{23} = 4.2209 \times 10^{-25} kg$ 
	- Volume of one unit cell = 4.7045881 x 10<sup>-29</sup>
	- d = 8971.9 kg m<sup>-3</sup>
## 3. Holes
- When atoms are packed together, there are gaps between atoms, known as holes.
	- Other atoms sit inside these holes (e.g. carbon inside iron) to form salts and alloys.
- Consider a FCC, or any arrangement with a **close-packing**.
	- Between the layers in FCC there are octahedral holes between atoms.
	- The size of the hole can be used to consider the best atom to fit the holes;
> The number of atoms is equivalent to the number of octahedral holes.
- Between crystals of three atoms capped by one atom, a tetrahedral hole forms.
> The number of atoms is equivalent to **half** the number of tetrahedral holes.
### 3.1. Calculating hole size
- As stated above, the hole size is essential for finding out the atom that fits.
#### 3.1.1. Octahedral holes
- Consider an octahedral hole.
- We define radius of hole as r<sub>h</sub>, and the radius of atom as r<sub>a</sub>.
	- $4r_a^2=2(r_a+r_h)^2$
	- $2r_a = \sqrt{2}(r_a+r_h) = r_a\sqrt{2}+r_h\sqrt{2}$
	- $r_a\sqrt{2} = r_a+r_h$
	- $r_h = 0.414...r_a$
#### 3.1.2. Tetrahedral holes
- Consider a tetrahedral hole.
- Again, we define radius of hole as r<sub>h</sub>, and the radius of atom as r<sub>a</sub>.
	- Consider a cube that forms trigonal bipyramidal:
		- The hole is at the middle, and side lengths of the cube is l.
	- $r_a+r_h = \frac{l\sqrt{3}}{2}$
	- $2r_a = l\sqrt{2}, \space l = \frac{2r_a}{\sqrt{2}} = r_a\sqrt{2}$
		- $r_h + r_a = \frac{r_a\sqrt{6}}{2}$
		- $r_h = \frac{r_a\sqrt{6}-2}{2}$
		- $r_h  \approx 0.225r_a$
