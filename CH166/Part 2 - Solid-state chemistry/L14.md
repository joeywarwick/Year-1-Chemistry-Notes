## 2. Carbonate stability (cont.)
- We can look at this energetically using a Born-Haber cycle for metal carbonates.
	1. Metal carbonate solid becomes gaseous metal cation and carbonate anion.:
		- ΔH<sub>L</sub>(MCO<sub>3</sub>), **positive**
	2. Gaseous carbonate ion decomposes to form carbon dioxide and oxide anion.
		- ΔH<sub>decomp</sub>(CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sub>), **positive**
	3. Gaseous metal cation and oxide anion combine to form **solid** metal oxide.
		- **-** ΔH<sub>L</sub>(MO), **negative** **(note the minus sign**)
	4. Solid metal oxide and carbon dioxide react to form solid metal carbonate.
		- Δ<sub>f</sub>(MCO<sub>3</sub>), **negative**.
- ΔH<sub>decomp</sub>(CO<sub>3</sub><sup>2-</sub>) is a constant, so the positive endothermic energies are **entirely dependent** on ΔH<sub>L</sub>(MCO<sub>3</sub>).
	- ΔH<sub>L</sub>(MCO<sub>3</sub>) is correlated with ΔH<sub>L</sub>(MO), so when the carbonate's LE increases the LE for the metal oxide also increases.
		- This **decreases** the Δ<sub>f</sub>(MCO<sub>3</sub>).
- Because Δ<sub>f</sub>(MCO<sub>3</sub>) decreases, **less energy** (heat) will be required to decompose the metal carbonate.
## 3. Defects
- Perfect crystals do not exist.
- Defects can be categorised into two types:
	- **Vacancy**, where a unit cell is missing an ion or counterion.
		- They **reduce lattice enthalpy**, because the lack of ions reduce the Madelung constant.
		- It linearly reduces Madelung. 
			- The more defects the more decrease.
	- **Other ions**, where an ion is substituted by another ion.
		- They increase the **lattice enthalpy**, because the distance between ions can either increase or decrease.
			- This affects the Madelung constant.
		- This also increases the **entropy** of the crystal greatly.
### 3.1. Energetics of substituting other ions
- When different ions are added, it increases the entropy of the crystals.
	- Initially, when the ions are added, it increases the entropy **greatly**. 
	- As more ions are added, the rate of increase **decreases**.
	- Past a certain number of ions, **the entropy decreases** again.
	- We can view this from a Gibbs perspective.
		- G = H - TS
		- Assuming constant temperature:
			- H increases with defect concentration
			- TS increases, but as we said above, not linearly, and gradually plateaus.
> **NOTE** There is a region from the start to equilibrium where TS > H.
- As a result, there is an equilibrium point between H and TS that results in **maximum stability**.
	- Hence a perfect crystal with no defects is **impossible**.
> Perfect crystals can exist when T=0, because at that point G = H.
### 3.2. Lithium-ion batteries
- Theoretical charge density of a mole of lithium-cobalt dioxide was calculated to be 900 C using theories from L8. But this is not true.
- The structure of these batteries involve lithium ions sandwiched between cobalt dioxide layers. 
	- When the battery is charged, electrons are pumped into the battery, so the Li+ picks it up and moves to the anode.
		- This increases the **entropy** of the system, because **vacancies** are being creates as the lithium ions move away.
		- The compensation provided by entropy is limited, so energy required to charge increases very quickly.
- Lithium batteries start as LiCoO<sub>2</sub>, but when half of the lithium is removed it becomes Li<sub>1/2</sub>CoO<sub>2</sub>, or LiCo<sub>2</sub>O<sub>4</sub>.
	- This structure is less preferable, as these AB<sub>2</sub>C<sub>4</sub> crystals prefer a **spinel** structure.
- So this disorders to a new structure in a very exothermic process, generating lots of heat.
