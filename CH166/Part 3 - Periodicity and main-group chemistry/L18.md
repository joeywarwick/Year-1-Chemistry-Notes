## 2. Group 13 elements
- Group 13 consists of both metals and non-metals.
### 2.1. Gallium
- Gallium is quite different to Al, In, and Tl.
	- It melts very easily;
	- It is arranged in pairs of Ga atoms, similar to solid iodine;
		- The **p-electrons** form the Ga-Ga bonds, and the **s-electrons** are delocalised.
			- 4s and 4p orbitals have very different energies.
		- This results in a very soft structure.
### 2.2. Ionisation energies
- There is still an overall decrease from B to Tl, but there are anomalies:
	- There is a general decreasing trend from B to Tl for **first ionisation energy**
	- But there is a significant increase from Al to Ga and from In to Tl in both 2nd and 3rd IE.
		- The graph resembles a 'W'
	- The first electron to be removed is the p-electron, which is quite easy to remove.
	- The second and third electrons removed are the s-electrons. And this means that the 4s and 6s pairs are very strongly bound to Ga and Tl.
		- It is also because that d- and f- orbitals are poorly shielding.
		- So the s-electrons are strongly held to the nucleus.
	- The strongly-bound s-pair is called the **inert** **pair**.
#### 2.2.1. Redox properties
- A consequence of IEs is the redox potential.
- This is a standard redox half equation:
	- $M^{3+} + 3e^- \rightarrow M$
- It becomes progressively less stable down the group, and in fact anything except Al<sup>3+</sup> is unstable.
	- They become **easier to reduce.**
- This is because the bonds formed cannot compensate for the high energy which arises from the high nuclear charge.
#### 2.2.2. Halides of Al - Tl
- As discussed, due to the stability of ions, not all group 13 metals form halides in the same way.

- All of the group 13 elements can form 3+ halides with any halogen.

	- Fittingly because two oxidation states exist in the compound.

### 2.3. Boron hydrides
- Borons can form a wide range of hydrides.
	- [BH<sub>4</sub>]<sup>-</sup>, tetraborohydrate
		- Isoelectric with CH<sub>4</sub>
	- Boron can form a range of web-like boranes with bridging hydrogens:
		- B<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> diborane
		- B<sub>n</sub>H<sub>n+4</sub>, nido- (nest-like)
		- B<sub>n</sub>H<sub>n+6</sub>, arachno- (web-like)
		- [B<sub></sub>H<sub>n</sub>]<sup>-</sup>, closo- (closed polyhedra)
#### 2.3.1. Diborane
- B<sub>2</sub>H<sub>6</sub> is a reactive gas at RTP.
- The two borons contain 6 valence electrons, but there are 8 bonds.
	- The terminal H is shorter and stronger and are held by one valence electon.
	- The middle bridging electrons are held by **less than one** electron.
- Only boron is capable of this, because Al-Tl do not form sufficiently strong bonds to support this.
