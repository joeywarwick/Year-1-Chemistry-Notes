## 2. Group 14 elements
- Their properties change dramatically from the top.
### 2.1. Electron configuration
- They will have the configuration of ns<sup>2</sup> np<sup>2</sup>.
	- They can be +4.
	- From the inert pair effect we will also know that 2+ is possible.
- They can also form anions with a electropositive ion.
### 2.2. Allotropes of carbon
- Carbon form a wide variety of allotropes.
	- **Diamond,** which consists of sp<sup>3</sup> carbon in a tetrahedral structures
	- **Graphite**, which consists of sp<sup>2</sup> carbon in a 2D layered structure.
		- Graphite is the **thermodynamically stable** form.
	- **C<sub>60</sub>** and other ball-shaped allotropes, which consists of a mix of sp<sup>3</sup> and sp<sup>2</sup>.
	- **Carbon nanotubes,** which are formed from rolled up layers of graphite.
### 2.3. Physical properties of group 14 elements
- From the top to the bottom, group 14 elements:
	- Move from giant covalent (diamond) towards metals (lead)
	- Conductivity increases from diamond to lead
		- C is nonmetal;
		- Si/Ge semiconductor;
		- Sn/Pb metal.
	- Melting/boiling point decreases
- Carbon:
	- Forms strong sigma bonds, energy levels well separated.
- Si/Ge:
	- Weaker sigma bonds;
		- Smaller separation between bonding and antibonding;
		- Thermal excitation of electrons possible
	- Conductivity increases with heat
- Sn/Pb:
	- Delocalised electrons.
#### 2.3. Ionic carbides
- Carbon will form carbides in the presence of electropositive ions.
	- CaC<sub>2</sub>, Na<sub>2</sub>C<sub>2</sub>
		- They have the negative ion [C=C]<sup>2-</sup>.
		- Calcium carbide forms a rock salt-type structure.
		- They will react to from ethene and calcium hydroxide:
			- $CaC_2 + 2H_2O \rightarrow C_2H_2 + Ca(OH)_2$
	- MgC<sub>3</sub>
		- They have the unusual [C=C=C]<sup>2-</sup> anion.
		- It is very reactive.
		- It will form polymers very easily.
### 2.4. Group 14 hydrides

| C                                      | Si                                            | Ge                                            | Sn                        |
| -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Alkanes, C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub> | Silanes, Si<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub>, n<10 | Germanes, Ge<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n+2</sub>, n<9 | Stannane, SnH<sub>4</sub> |
| Alkenes, C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n</sub>   |                                               |                                               |                           |
| Alkynes, C<sub>n</sub>H<sub>2n-2</sub> |                                               |                                               |                           |
- C-C bonds are quite strong, so pi bonds are possible.
- Si-Si pi bonds are weak.
	- Because it is between two large atoms.
	- Protecting groups can stabilise the reactive Si-Si bonds.
- Overall bonds decrease in strength down the group, so it becomes harder to form long chains.
	- However, Si-O bonds are quite strong, stronger than C-O, due to the extra polarity of the bond.
		- So silane will become silicon dioxide in water.
			- $SiH_4 + 2H_2O \rightarrow SiO_2 + 4H_2$
### 2.5. Group 14 oxides
#### 2.5.1. Carbon oxides
- Carbon forms a range of oxides:
	- Monoxides, CO
	- Dioxides, CO<sub>2</sub>
	- C<sub>3</sub>O<sub>2</sub>
		- This is unstable and polymerises easily.
#### 2.5.2. Silicon and germanium oxides
- Silicon dioxide is a solid, aka quartz.
	- Silicon dioxide has four oxygens **double bonded** to each silicon, and each oxygen is in turn shared between two silicons.
- When SiO<sub>2</sub> structure is disrupted it forms tetrahedrons connected by the corners.
	- They will carry a negative charge.
#### 2.5.3. Tin and lead oxides
- Tin and lead are octahedral close-packed ionic structures.
	- As they are quite large they will have high coordination number.
- The octahedra share corners to form a large ionic structure.
- Oxidation state becomes less stable going down the group, but they are stabilised by the strong bonds formed with oxygen, so 4+ is possible.
	- SnO<sub>2</sub> and PbO<sub>2</sub>.
> **NOTE** Neither C or Si will have 2+. The bonds are sufficiently strong to directly form 4+.
- +2 is also possible to leave behind the inert pair:
	- SnO/PbO
	- They are not octahedral or tetrahedral, due to the lone pair.
		- It has multiple layers of a pyramidal structure.
	- Lead in particular can form 'red lead', Pb<sub>3</sub>O<sub>4</sub>. 
		- This is **multivalent**. 
			- $(Pb^{4+})(Pb^{2+})_2O_4$
			- Because 4+ is octahedral and 2+ is pyramidal, the structure is unique.
> The red colour arises from the transition between 4+ AND 2+.
