## 2. Basic concepts of polymers
- Polymers are large molecules.
	- They encompass things like plastics, proteins, DNA, and carbohydrates.
- We generally consider molecules with molecular weight (M<sub>w</sub>) above 10000 as polymers.
	- At M<sub>w</sub> >5000 oligomer is a frequently used term.
- They also tend to have a structural repeat unit.
### 2.1. Properties of polymers
#### 2.1.1. Defining polymers.
- If more than 50% consists of one molecular species, it is **not** a polymer.
	- Polymers have **varied** M<sub>w</sub>.
- As a result, they will have a molecular weight distribution (MWD)
- They will also have a polydispersity index (PDI).
	- M<sub>n</sub> is **number-average** molecular weight
	- M<sub>w</sub> is **weight-average** molecular weight.
	- The dispersity is defined as $\frac{M_w}{M_n}$ 
		- It's value is greater than 1.
> Polymer is officially defined as a molecule with >3 monomer units.
	- Take ethylene for example.
		- Repeat unit C<sub>2</sub>H<sub>4</sub>.
			- So we would need a chain of C4H8, C6H12, and C8H16.
	- The above composition satisfies that there is less that 50% of each M<sub>w</sub> and that the average has at least three units.
### 2.2. Properties regarding MWt
- Ethane is a gas, M<sub>w</sub> 28.
- Octane is a liquid, M<sub>w</sub> 114.
- Wax is a solid, M<sub>w</sub> 306.
	- As the M<sub>w</sub> increases the density, mp/bp increases.
