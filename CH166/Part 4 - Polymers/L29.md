## 2. Thermal FRP
- Free radicals can be produced by heating an initiator, such that it decays thermally.
- It consists of three steps:
	- Initiation
	- Propagation
	- Termination
- The attack is nucleophilic, done by a single electron on the free radical.
	- The EWGs stabilise the extra electron.
- A similar method is anionic polymerisation, which replaces the free radical with an anion.
- Theoretically any alkene can undergo FRP, but a EWG is preferred due to its stabilising properties.
### 2.1. Thermal initiation
- A common initiator is AIBN, or benzoyl peroxide.
- It decays in a first-order reaction to from a free radical.
	- This decay is **extremely** dependent on temperature **and** concentration of initiator.
		- $Rate = k[initiator]$
- The initiator's concentration decreases as the reaction progresses. The time taken for the concentration to halve is the **half-life**. 
	- The half-life increases as the temperature decreases.
### 2.2. Propagation
- The radical attacks the monomer, forming monomer radicals.
- This step is second-order.
	- $Rate = k[R^\bullet][M]$
		- [R*], concentration of radical, is somewhat **constant,** due to regular rate.
		- [M], concentration of monomer, should decrease as more polymer chains form.
### 2.3. Termination
- There are two ways termination can occur:
	- Combination:
		- Two oligomer radicals combine to form one polymer chain.
		- $R^\bullet + R^\bullet \rightarrow R-R$
			- Second-order:
			- $Rate = k[R^\bullet]^2$
	- Disproportionation:
		- Two polymer chains form separate polymer chains.
		- $R^\bullet + R^\bullet \rightarrow R-X + R-X$
			- Also second-order
			- $Rate = k[R^\bullet]^2$
> NMR can be used to follow the reaction.
### 2.4. Kinetic chain length
- The kinetic chain length is defined as the number of chain growth per effective radical.
	- It's the ratio of propagation rate to initiation rate.
- Where there is no transfer process it is equal to the number average degree of polymerisation
	- Termination is done only through disproportionation. 
### 2.5. Ceiling temperature
- Polymerisation processes are reversible.
	- At temperatures below the ceiling temperature, T<sub>c</sub>, polymerisation dominates.
	- At temperatures above T<sub>c</sub>, depolymerisation dominates.
> This is useful for recycling polymers.
