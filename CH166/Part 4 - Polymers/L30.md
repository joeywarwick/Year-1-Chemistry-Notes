## 2. Condensation polymerisation
- It is a type of step-growth polymerisation.
- There are two types:
	- AB + AB = ABAB
		- Hydroxycarboxylic acids
	- AA + BB = AABB
		- Diacids with diols
> This is the same mechanism through with polyurea and polyurethanes are made.
- Condensation polymers are generally step-growth polymers.
### 2.1. Conversion rate
-    If we had one mole of a diol and one mole of a diacid, this would yield one mole of ester.
	- This is 50% conversion.
- If the ester reacted with another mole of diol, this yields one mole of diester.
	- But this is 75% conversion.
- Conversion is defined as: $\frac{Number \space of \space reacted \space functional \space groups}{Total \space number \space of \space functional \space groups}$
	- It is notates as p, where p>0<1
- At 50-75% yield no viable quantities of polymer forms.
- So a very high yield is required.
#### 2.1.1. Caruthers equation
- $M_n = DP \cdot M_0$
	- M<sub>n</sub> is number-average molecular weight.
	- DP is the number of repeat units.
	- M<sub>0</sub> is the weight of each repeat unit.
- By substituting the fact that molecules decrease by one for every monomer added:
	- Take number of monomers as N<sub>0</sub> and the number of molecules as N:
		- Groups reacted = $N_0 - N$
	- So $p = \frac{N_0 - N}{N}$
		- $\frac{N}{N_0} = 1-p$
- By further substituting the formula for DP:
	- $DP = \frac{monomers}{molecules} = \frac{N_0}{N} = \frac{1}{1-p}$
	- $M_n = \frac{M_0}{(1-p)}$
- Similarly, we can derive M<sub>w</sub>, dispersity, and M<sub>0</sub> from this.
> M<sub>0</sub> is the mass of the repeat unit.
- For M<sub>w</sub>;
	- $M_w = M_0\frac{1+p}{1-p}$
- For dispersity:
	- 1 + p
- To determine M<sub>0</sub> a graph and be plotted to determine graphically.
### 2.2. Examples of condensation polymers
- Polyamides
	- Nylon
		- Clothing, fabrics
- Polyesters
	- PET
		- Bottles, containers
- Polyethers
	- PEG
		- Surfactant, cosmetics
- Polyurethanes
	- Polyurethanes have a R-NCO group, which polymerizes into **RNHCOOR
	- PU
		- Foams.
> **NOTE** Polyurethane has no elimination of a small molecule.
#### 2.2.1. Special condensation polymerisations
- Not all polymerisations are AABB or ABAB.
- A cyclic monomer can be used in a **ring-opening polymerisation** to form a polymer.
	- Common example is polycaprolactone, polylactic acid, polyglycolic acid
#### 2.2.2. Biodegradable polymers
- Examples include Poly(hydroxy butyrate).
- It can be decomposed by bacteria in a fermentation tank.
