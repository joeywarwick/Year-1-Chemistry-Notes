## 2. Polymers in solution
- A polymer which is dissolved in a suitable solvent forms a random coil.
	- The solvent gradually diffuses into the chains of the polymers.
		- This swells and softens the polymers.
		- Initially a gel is formed, which then turns into a solution.
		- The solutions will have a viscosity.
	- So the polymer chains loosen and tangle.
- The coils roughly represent a sphere. Using this sphere we can deinfe:
	- The hydrodynamic volume;
		- Average volume of the sphere.
	- The gyration radius, R<sub>g</sub>.
		- Average radius of the sphere.
> **NOTE** The volume of the spheres is related to the solubility of the polymer in the solvent.
### 2.1. Kinematic viscosity
- This can be measured using a viscometer.
	- Using measurements and calculations we reach an **intrinsic viscosity**.
	- We use this value to find kinematic viscosity.
- The value of the kinematic viscosity is calculated by the Mark-Houwink equation.
	- $[\eta] = kM^a$
		- This allows us to relate viscosity to molecular weight.
		- k is a constant dependent on a range of factors.
- We can then further relate this to hydrodynamic volume:
	- $\log{(M \cdot [\eta])} \propto \log{V_h}$
### 2.2. Mass numbers for polymers
- GPC can be used to determine molecular weight.
- We need two masses:
	- Number-average molecular mass, M<sub>n</sub> and
	- Weight-average molecular mass, M<sub>w</sub>.
- To obtain these masses, we need a couple of quantities.
	- M<sub>i</sub>, mass of a chain
	- N<sub>i</sub>, number of chains with a particular mass.
- From here, we use these two numbers to calculate the two masses.
	- $M_n = \frac{\Sigma N_iM_i}{\Sigma N_i}$
	- $M_w = \frac{\Sigma N_i{M_i}^2}{\Sigma N_iM_i}$
- Another metrics is dispersity.
	- Dispersity measures the variation in molecular weight.
	- It is given as the ratio of WAM to NAM:
		- $D = \frac{M_w}{M_n}$
- In a GPC trace, polymer chains are separated by size, rather than weight.
