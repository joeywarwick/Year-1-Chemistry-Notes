## 2. Polyolefins
### 2.1. Polyethylene, PE
- The simplest polyolefin is **polyethylene**.
- It is classified into different types based on density and branching.
	- More branching = Lower density
- As discussed previously, the mechanical properties of PE depend highly on parameters like:
	- M<sub>w</sub>
	- Crystal structure
- So it is grouped into many different classes:
	- Ultra-high MW PE, UHMWPE
		- Fibres. automobile parts
	- Ultra-low MW PE, ULMWPE/PE-WAX
		- Wax polishes, spray paint
	- High MW PE, HMWPE
		- Chopping boards
	- High-density PE, HDPE
		-  Bottles, trays
	- Linear low-density PE, LLDPE
		- Wire insulation
	- Low-density PE, LDPE.
		- Films, bags
- Addition polymers are generally chain-growth, where it forms smaller oligomeric chains then merge to from a polymer.
#### 2.1.1. LDPE
- Ethene at 20k-40k psi and 150-325 C<sup>o</sup> will yield LDPE in a free radial polymerisation process.
- It has highly branched chains.
- T<sub>m</sub> ~105C, MWt 20k -100k.
### 2.2. Polymerisation routes
- Ethene was initially considered non-polymerisable, until it was compressed at very high temperatures, which resulted in a white solid.
	- This is a type of Free Radical Polymerisation.
- Nowadays it is synthesised using TM catalysts.
	- This is called the Ziegler-Natta polymerisation.
> The ligand on the TM catalyst can affect stereochemistry.
#### 2.2.1. Ziegler-Natta Polymerisation
- The monomer starts by coordinating to a TM. 
- The TM undergoes a 16/18-electron catalysis
- Forms oligomers which merge to form a polymer.
### 2.3. Polypropylene
