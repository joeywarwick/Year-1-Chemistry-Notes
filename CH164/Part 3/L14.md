## 2. Conjugation
- Conjugated systems are **systems where π-bonding interactions are present over at least three adjacent atoms.** In organic molecules, this is typically observed as a **planar sequence of multiple sp<sup>2</sup> hybridised atoms.**
	- sp HAOs may not satisfy requirements for conjugation. 
	- Take CH<sub>2</sub>=C=CH<sub>2</sub>, while all can form π-bonds, the central carbon has p-orbitals that are perpendicular to each other
		- So electronic movement between the two π-bonds becomes difficult
	- Hence it is not a conjugated system.
>**NOTE** Conjugated systems may involve more than carbon. Oxygen and nitrogen are common heteroatoms involved in conjugated systems.
- Conjugation stabilises molecules.
### 2.1. 1,3-butadiene
- Traditional Lewis structures do not represent the experimental observations fully.
- π-bonding MOs should be considered.
#### 2.1.1. Disagreements between Lewis structure and experimental observations
- Lewis structure portrays 1,3-butadiene as four carbons connected by alternating single and double bonds.
- There is greater rotational barrier around the central 'single' bond than expected for a C-C bond
- The bond length of the central single bond is longer than expected and the bond lengths of the double bonds are shorter than expected.

| Isolated bond | Length / angstroms |
| ------------- | ------------------ |
| C-C           | 1.54               |
| C=C           | 1.34               |

| 1,3-butadiene bond | Length / angstroms |
| ------------------ | ------------------ |
| C-C                | 1.45               |
| C=C                | 1.37               |

> Recall that bond length is negatively related to strength, and strength is positively related to bond order.
